DEMOCRACY SIMULATOR — ENVIRONMENT AND SCENARIO LIBRARY
======================================================

PURPOSE
-------
This file is a design cue for Codex. Democracy Simulator must be a reusable civic-simulation platform, not a single drainage-repair game. Players should be able to choose or generate environments inspired by different regions, political systems, administrative cultures, economies, and civic problems.

Use fictional countries, cities, parties, officials, and institutions by default. Real-world systems may inspire mechanics, but scenarios should remain configurable and generalized.

CORE ENVIRONMENT FORMULA
------------------------
Every generated environment should combine:

1. World profile
2. Political system
3. Administrative culture
4. Legal system
5. Economic conditions
6. Social conditions
7. Media environment
8. Player role
9. Civic objective
10. Institutions
11. Stakeholders
12. Available actions
13. Hidden variables
14. Procedural barriers
15. Random events
16. Success and failure conditions
17. Ethical trade-offs
18. Visual theme
19. Time horizon
20. Persistent consequences

The same objective must behave differently under different environments.

Example: acquiring land in a transparent, digitized democracy should depend on planning law, financing, and consultation. The same objective in a patronage-heavy environment may depend on brokers, political access, disputed records, and informal payments. In a fragile democracy, displacement, local conflict, or sudden policy changes may dominate.

GENERATION RULES
================

Generate each environment by selecting:
- 1 WORLD_REGION
- 1 GOVERNMENT_LEVEL
- 1 POLITICAL_SYSTEM
- 1 ADMINISTRATIVE_CAPACITY
- 1 CORRUPTION_STRUCTURE
- 1 RULE_OF_LAW
- 1 MEDIA_ENVIRONMENT
- 1 ECONOMIC_CONDITION
- 1 PLAYER_ROLE
- 1 CIVIC_OBJECTIVE_TYPE
- 2–6 institutions
- 3–8 stakeholders
- 6–15 actions
- 2–6 procedural barriers
- 3–10 random events
- 1–4 environment modifiers

Validate combinations for coherence.

Examples:
- A village drainage mission should not immediately require a constitutional court.
- A minister-resignation mission should include media, parliament, party leadership, and protest mechanics.
- A business-land mission should include registry, zoning, financing, and community interests.
- A rights case should include legal standing, evidence, public support, and court access.

-------------------
World: South Asian megacity

Institutions: Municipality, ward office, public works department, local court
Stakeholders: Engineer, councillor, residents, contractor, journalist

Institutions: Land registry, municipality, environmental agency, utility, tax office
Stakeholders: Landowners, community leader, bank, broker, environmental official

IMPLEMENTATION CUE FOR CODEX
============================

1. Keep scenarios data-driven.
2. Store abstractions separately from scenario instances.
3. Use JSON or YAML plus strongly typed validation.
4. Do not embed political assumptions in rendering code.
5. Let players select region, political environment, role, category, and difficulty.
6. Include a random-world option.
7. Generate visuals from theme packs.
8. Keep probabilities and hidden variables server-side.
9. Preserve game history across missions.
10. Let earlier choices affect later scenarios.
11. Add a scenario validator.
12. Add a balancing/Monte Carlo tool.
13. Implement three complete starter environments first.
14. Add future scenarios through data packs without engine changes.
15. Reuse the same event, resource, persistence, animation, and UI systems.

FINAL DEVELOPMENT GOAL
======================

Build Democracy Simulator as a reusable world-scale civic simulation platform.

Players must be able to select or generate different countries, cities, political systems, civic problems, roles, institutions, and difficulty levels. The engine should combine reusable abstractions to create distinct environments rather than hard-coding individual missions.

The first release should contain three complete playable environments:
1. Neighborhood drainage repair
2. Education-minister resignation campaign
3. Land acquisition for a new business

All three must share the same engine, event system, resource model, persistence, visual framework, and scenario-generation architecture.
